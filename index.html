Why are the centroid and volume bisector different?

The centroid is determined by the mean height of the solid's volume distribution, whereas the volume-bisecting plane lies at its median. For a paraboloid of revolution, these heights are 2H/3 and H/√2, respectively. The distinction is geometric, not numerical error.

About this experiment

For a homogeneous solid of height H whose horizontal sectional area is A(z) = Czp, the centroid is the mean height and the volume-bisecting plane lies at the median height. The case p = 1 includes a truncated paraboloid; p = 2 includes a cone. For p > 2, the area profile is mathematically valid but cannot come from a three-dimensional convex solid.

This laboratory is a companion to the expository manuscript Bisecting Is Not Balancing: Means, Medians, and a Paraboloid. Source code, mathematical notes, and citation information are maintained in the MeanMedianLab repository.
